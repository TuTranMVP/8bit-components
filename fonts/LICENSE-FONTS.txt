Subset — reproduce either mono face exactly with:

  pyftsubset IoskeleyMono-Regular.ttf \
    --unicodes="U+0000-024F,U+0259,U+1E00-1EFF,U+2000-206F,U+20AB,U+2190-21FF,U+25A0-25FF" \
    --layout-features="ccmp,locl,calt,dlig,zero,onum,lnum,frac,numr,dnom" \
    --flavor=woff2 --output-file=nes-mono-400.woff2

That range is Latin + Vietnamese + punctuation + ₫ + arrows + geometric shapes, and
it matches the `unicode-range` in tokens.css — the two have to agree or the browser
downloads a face it then cannot use.

The feature list is deliberate, not `*`. Iosevka ships 24 private cherry-picking
tags (APLF, HSKL, JLIA, MTLB, PHPX, RAKU, SWFT, WFLM …) that swap in
language-specific glyph shapes and only ever fire behind an explicit
font-feature-settings. Nothing in this library asks for them, so they and their 346
unreachable alternates are dropped: 67.4 kB → 54.8 kB per face, with every one of
the 1088 subset codepoints keeping its exact outline and 600-unit advance. What is
kept is what actually renders: ccmp and locl (Vietnamese mark composition), calt and
dlig (the code ligatures), and the numeric set.

Vietnamese coverage is asserted, not assumed — `pnpm check:ui` measures every
diacritic at 0.6em in a real browser, which a fallback glyph cannot fake.
